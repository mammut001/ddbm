\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We rebuilt the project from its first report, retrained every model and evaluated on the full-resolution SID test set and on held-out ELD scenes. The answers to the three questions are more nuanced than the first version suggested.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Bottleneck refinement with FiLM helps; teacher-latent distillation does not.} The Bridge U-Net without distillation is the most accurate single network (28.94\,dB, +0.32\,dB over our SID U-Net), and FiLM conditioning on the exposure ratio and noise level contributes 0.25\,dB. Distillation towards the encoder's own long-exposure features constrains the bridge and costs 0.58\,dB.
\item \textbf{A new camera needs very little data, and meta-learning helps only when adaptation is short.} One scene of an ELD camera adds about 2\,dB over the zero-shot model, and five scenes match full fine-tuning. Once support and query are separated by scene, MAML gives the best starting point and the best result after a few inner steps. With a longer adaptation the plain SID U-Net is as good or better.
\item \textbf{A real diffusion bridge loses on fidelity and wins on a perceptual metric; averaged with the U-Net, it gains on both.} The feature-space variant of the first version is not a diffusion model and breaks under its own sampler. A simplified pixel-space DDBM reaches 28.03\,dB in 5 steps, and more steps lower PSNR, as expected for posterior sampling. On LPIPS it beats the SID U-Net by 14\% (0.394 vs.\ 0.459), the perception--distortion trade-off in action. The VE recipe of the released code trains far faster when its bridge noise is matched to the data range, but after 60k iterations and with 20 steps it stays below the simplified bridge on every metric. The full DDBM, with the released VP bridge and sampler and \VPITERS{} iterations, is the clearest case of the trade-off: LPIPS 37\% below the U-Net (\VPFORTYLPIPS{}) and the sharpest images, for 1.3\,dB less PSNR, 0.10 less SSIM, about 300 times the run time and some invented detail. A faithful diffusion bridge therefore does not replace regression for low-light enhancement, but it complements it. Averaging the two outputs, as the theory of~\cite{freirich2021wasserstein} suggests, beats the U-Net on PSNR and LPIPS at once (\BLENDQPSNR\,dB and \BLENDQLPIPS{} at $w=\BLENDQW$) and the DDBM on every metric ($w=\BLENDLW$). Starting the bridge from the U-Net output, the other way to combine the two, gives no gain (\VPUNETPSNR{} vs.\ \VPFORTYPSNR\,dB). Averaging samples is a slow way to reach the regression network's PSNR: four samples of each DDBM give \MSEIGHTPSNR\,dB against the U-Net's \MSUNETPSNR\,dB on 100 test pairs, with a minute per image. The useful fidelity output of the bridge is its one-step estimate. Averaged over the two DDBMs it beats the U-Net on all four metrics (\STEPAVGPSNR\,dB and LPIPS \STEPAVGLPIPS{}) with three network evaluations. Part of that gain is the ensemble effect of two networks, but a control with a second SID U-Net measures it at \GABA\,dB (the U-Net pair reaches \UABPSNR\,dB and an unchanged LPIPS), about a quarter of the \GDDA\,dB gain of the DDBM pair. On three other cameras, with no adaptation, that pair is \XPAIRAABS\,dB below the U-Net, because the DDBM with the naive start does not transfer, while the DDBM started from the U-Net output keeps a gain of \XUSA\,dB and the best LPIPS. Adapting only the U-Net to a new camera with a few scenes and feeding its output to the unadapted DDBM keeps the U-Net's PSNR and lowers LPIPS by about \FSDULSIX{} (six scenes), whereas adapting the DDBM with its bridge loss gains little; an L1 loss on its one-step estimate adapts it better (LPIPS as low, PSNR still below the adapted U-Net's).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Limitations.} Every configuration was trained once. A second run of the SID U-Net differs by only \SIDSEEDGAP\,dB, but two runs of one model are a weak variance estimate, so the smaller gaps in Table~\ref{tab:sid} (for example FiLM, 0.25\,dB) need more seeds to be firm. The cloud machine running the first set of experiments was reclaimed before all files were copied. We kept every number reported here, but lost the checkpoints. The qualitative results and the LPIPS scores therefore cover only the models trained afterwards (the SID U-Net and four pixel-space DDBMs), and the $\sigma_{\max}=80$ DDBM has no test score. The full DDBM follows the released code in everything but the network, so our numbers do not cover the ADM U-Net with attention. The few-shot comparison of the U-Net and the DDBM uses one seed (the comparison of adaptation recipes uses three), two adaptation objectives for the DDBM (the bridge loss and an L1 loss on its one-step estimate) and three cameras. The cross-camera test uses 12 ELD scenes, and the failure of the naive-start DDBM there goes with a start image 2.8 to 4.3 times brighter than the target (1.2 times on SID), but we did not test whether the brightness alone causes it. The blend weakens the invented detail of the DDBM but does not remove it. The sample-averaging study uses 100 test pairs, two per scene, and the ensemble control uses a single second seed and equal numbers of networks, not equal training budgets. LPIPS is one learned metric; a user study or a no-reference measure would make the perceptual comparison firmer.

\paragraph{Future work.} The strongest single result is that one or a few scenes are enough to adapt to a new sensor. This makes on-device adaptation feasible: a phone could capture a handful of short/long RAW pairs of a static scene and fine-tune the decoder and bridge only. Combining this with calibrated noise synthesis~\cite{wei2020eld}, and distilling the diffusion bridge into one or two steps, are the natural next steps. The VP bridge did not profit from starting at the U-Net output, so the next bridge to try is one with much less noise, such as posterior-mean rectified flow~\cite{ohayon2025pmrf}.
