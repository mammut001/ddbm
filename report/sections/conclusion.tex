\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We rebuilt the project from its first report, retrained every model and evaluated on the full-resolution SID test set and on held-out ELD scenes. The answers to the three questions are more nuanced than the first version suggested.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Bottleneck refinement with FiLM helps; teacher-latent distillation does not.} The Bridge U-Net without distillation is the most accurate model (28.94\,dB, +0.32\,dB over our SID U-Net), and FiLM conditioning on the exposure ratio and noise level contributes 0.25\,dB. Distillation towards the encoder's own long-exposure features constrains the bridge and costs 0.58\,dB.
\item \textbf{A new camera needs very little data, and meta-learning helps only when adaptation is short.} One scene of an ELD camera adds about 2\,dB over the zero-shot model, and five scenes match full fine-tuning. Once support and query are separated by scene, MAML gives the best starting point and the best result after a few inner steps. With a longer adaptation the plain SID U-Net is as good or better.
\item \textbf{A real diffusion bridge loses on fidelity metrics and wins on a perceptual one.} The feature-space variant of the first version is not a diffusion model and breaks under its own sampler. A simplified pixel-space DDBM reaches 28.03\,dB in 5 steps, and more steps lower PSNR, as expected for posterior sampling. On LPIPS it beats the SID U-Net by 14\% (0.394 vs.\ 0.459), the perception--distortion trade-off in action. The VE recipe of the released code trains far faster when its bridge noise is matched to the data range, but after 60k iterations and with 20 steps it stays below the simplified bridge on every metric. The full DDBM, with the released VP bridge and sampler and \VPITERS{} iterations, is the clearest case of the trade-off: the best LPIPS (\VPFORTYLPIPS{}, 37\% below the U-Net) and the sharpest images, for 1.3\,dB less PSNR, 0.10 less SSIM, about 300 times the run time and some invented detail. A faithful diffusion bridge therefore does not replace regression for low-light enhancement. It gives a different output, closer on a perceptual metric and further away pixel by pixel.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Limitations.} Every configuration was trained once. A second run of the SID U-Net differs by only \SIDSEEDGAP\,dB, but two runs of one model are a weak variance estimate, so the smaller gaps in Table~\ref{tab:sid} (for example FiLM, 0.25\,dB) need more seeds to be firm. The cloud machine running the first set of experiments was reclaimed before all files were copied. We kept every number reported here, but lost the checkpoints. The qualitative results and the LPIPS scores therefore cover only the four models trained afterwards (the SID U-Net and three pixel-space DDBMs), and the $\sigma_{\max}=80$ DDBM has no test score. The full DDBM follows the released code in everything but the network, so our numbers do not cover the ADM U-Net with attention. LPIPS is one learned metric; a user study or a no-reference measure would make the perceptual comparison firmer.

\paragraph{Future work.} The strongest single result is that one or a few scenes are enough to adapt to a new sensor. This makes on-device adaptation feasible: a phone could capture a handful of short/long RAW pairs of a static scene and fine-tune the decoder and bridge only. Combining this with calibrated noise synthesis~\cite{wei2020eld}, and distilling the diffusion bridge into one or two steps, are the natural next steps.
