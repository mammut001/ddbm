\begin{abstract}
Images taken in extreme low light with a short exposure are dominated by noise and colour shifts. The SID U-Net, trained on RAW short/long exposure pairs, is a strong baseline, but it is tied to one camera and outputs a regression average. We revisit three ways to improve it, re-implemented from scratch and evaluated on the full-resolution SID Sony test set and on three ELD cameras. (1) A \emph{Bridge U-Net} refines the bottleneck with FiLM blocks conditioned on exposure ratio and noise level. With teacher-latent distillation it is slightly worse than the baseline (28.36 vs.\ 28.62\,dB); without distillation it is the most accurate model (28.94\,dB), and FiLM adds 0.25\,dB. (2) For new cameras, fine-tuning on six scenes recovers about 3\,dB, and five scenes per camera are as good. We fix a leak in the original meta-learning setup, where support and query shared targets, and build scene-disjoint tasks with simulated sensor responses. The meta-learned initialisation is best without adaptation and with short adaptation (+0.9 to +1.2\,dB), but a plain SID U-Net adapts best with 200 Adam steps. (3) The feature-space ``DDBM'' of the first version collapses under its own iterative inference (18.0\,dB). A simplified pixel-space DDBM reaches 28.03\,dB, below regression on PSNR as theory predicts, but it is 14\% better on the perceptual metric LPIPS (0.394 vs.\ 0.459). The released DDBM recipe learns very slowly with its default noise scale ($\sigma_{\max}=80$: 24.1\,dB on validation after 60k iterations). The full DDBM, with the released VP bridge and 40-step sampler and \VPITERS{} iterations, lowers LPIPS by 37\% (\VPFORTYLPIPS{}) and gives the sharpest images, but it falls to \VPFORTYPSNR\,dB and SSIM \VPFORTYSSIM{}, and part of its detail is invented. Averaging its output with the U-Net's beats the U-Net on PSNR and LPIPS at once (\BLENDQPSNR\,dB and \BLENDQLPIPS{}, weight chosen on validation), and with more weight on the DDBM it beats the DDBM on every metric.
\end{abstract}
